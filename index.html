Learn through Play! ✨

Discover simple, engaging activities like drawing, storytelling, puzzles, and pretend play that build essential early childhood skills for ages 3 to 6.

Target Age 3 – 6 Years Old
Proposed Solution Educational Video
Primary Goal Caregiver Support
Overview

1. Focus Problem

What is the Problem?

Limited awareness among parents and caregivers regarding age-appropriate play activities suitable for children ages 3 to 6 and their developmental benefits.

Why is it Important?

Play activities—such as drawing, storytelling, puzzles, and pretend play—are frequently viewed merely as entertainment rather than essential tools for developmental growth.

Who is Affected?

Children ages 3–6, parents, legal guardians, babysitting relatives, and informal caregivers (babysitters and daycare staff).

Diagnostic

2. Root Cause Analysis — 5 Whys

Click each step to examine the reasoning:

They may not have enough information about which play activities are suitable for children ages 3–6.
Information about age-appropriate play and its developmental benefits may not always be accessible or easy to understand.
Activities, such as drawing, storytelling, puzzles, and pretend play, may often be viewed mainly as a form of entertainment.
Parents may not know which specific skills these activities can help develop.
Parents may have difficulty choosing play activities that match their children’s developmental needs.
Final Root Cause

"Limited access to clear and understandable information about age-appropriate play and its developmental benefits."

Target Audience

3. Target Audience & Caregiver Needs

Who is the Audience?

The caregiving system surrounding young children: parents, legal guardians, extended family members, babysitters, and daycare attendants who facilitate play but lack formal early milestone training.

Demographics

Parents and caregivers with children aged 3 to 6 years old, particularly those seeking guidance on age-appropriate play to avoid unrealistic developmental expectations.

Caregiver Needs

Knowing suitable activities helps support learning, physical development, and social skills while letting children explore preferred interests without being forced.

Concerns to Avoid

Safety Risks
Delayed Growth
Lack of Interest
Too Much Pressure
Proposed Solution

4. YouTube Educational Video & Activity Guide

Educational Demonstration Solution Video
Simple and fun activities for home play (Ages 3–6). Watch on YouTube

What Will the Video Contain?

A short explanation of age-appropriate play, followed by simple activities suitable for ages 3–6: drawing and coloring, storytelling, puzzles, and pretend play. It explains how these support language, creativity, problem-solving, coordination, and social skills.

How It Addresses the Problem

It provides parents and caregivers with clear, accessible, and reliable information through visual demonstration, showing exactly how everyday play routines foster early developmental milestones.

Explore Play Activities & Skill Benefits

Click an activity to see its primary developmental benefits:

Drawing & Coloring

Develops fine motor control, hand-eye coordination, pencil grip, color identification, and early creative self-expression.

Fine Motor Skills Creativity Physical Coordination
